A

The station

A weather station that thinks in paddocks.

Forecasts are written for towns. Your herd lives forty kilometres from the nearest one, on ground where a front can cross a ridge and drop the temperature twelve degrees in an hour.

Halden puts one nine-metre mast on your range and one console on your desk. The mast watches the sky the way a stockman does: how fast the cloud is building, which way the wind is backing, how wet the grass is underfoot. The console turns that into one plain instruction: which paddock, and by when.

B

Instruments

Four modules. One instruction.

Each module does one job and shows its work on a readout you can check at a glance, with gloves on, from across the room.

  • 1

    Cloud build

    Index 0.72 · Rising

    Sees towers forming

    A sky camera scores vertical growth every ninety seconds, so a cell that will matter by dusk is flagged at lunch.

  • 2

    Wind shear

    14 kt NW · Backing

    Knows the lee side

    Two anemometers at three and nine metres catch the shift early and name the paddock that will be sheltered when it lands.

  • 3

    Pasture moisture

    31% · Firm footing

    Reads the ground too

    Buried probes tell you which gates will be mud after the rain, so the route you pick is one the herd can walk.

  • 4

    Herd position

    Herd B · 48 head

    Counts who is where

    Collar pings place every group on the map. The move window is worked out from how far they have to go.
